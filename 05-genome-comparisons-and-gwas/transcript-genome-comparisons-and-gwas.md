## Slide 15 — Read trimming

Read trimming
- Read trimming is the process of shortening the reads
- Removal of low-quality reads
- Removal of known adapter sequences

- Illumina sequencing technology relies on adapter
sequences. They have two functions
- Attachment of your shortened genome to surface
- Provide template for standard primer
- Provide clear end point of read

Illumina technologies

- In the sequencing process your genome fragment is flanked by adapter sequences
- During the sequencing process, the adapter on the opposite side of your DNA fragment is
sequenced
- These are known sequences at the end of your reads, they need to be trimmed off
- BBDuk, trimmomatic, cutadapt

Example run BBDuk to remove adapters
bbduk.sh in=reads.fq out=clean.fq ref=adapters.fa ktrim=r

Example run BBDuk to remove low quality reads
bbduk.sh in=reads.fq out=clean.fq maq=20

## Slide 32 — Genome comparisons terminology

Genome comparisons terminology
- Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs)
- Single nucleotide changes that represent at least 1% of the species’ population
- Historically used for wild strains or known alterations common within a species
- For example, your blood type

- Single nucleotide variants (SNVs)
- Single nucleotide changes, period. Marta’s lab-made UV mutant contains SNVs but would
officially not be considered SNPs.

- Genomic variants
- All genomic changes, this includes larger deletions, insertions and genome re-arrangements.
This also includes SNVs and is therefore the safest but least precise term.

## Slide 65 — Bulked segregant analysis

Bulked segregant analysis
- If you have two (in this case yeast)
strains
- One has phenotype of interest
- One does not have phenotype
of interest

- Perform mating with the strains
- Test ‘children’ or segregants for phenotype
- Group all positive segregants and all négatives
in separate pools
- Pool 1 is positive
- Pool 2 is negative

## Slide 66 — Sequencing and thought process

Sequencing and thought process
- Mix segregants within each pools
- Collect a tube containing the genomes of all the segregants
of a single pool (typically 50 segregants per pool)

- Sequence the two genome mixtures
- Reads obtained will represent the whole pool
- For SNVs unassociated with the phenotype should
be 50% of positive reads have it and 50% of negatives have it
- For SNV causal of phenotype should be in all 100% of positive
reads and 0% of negative reads

Example two phenotypes rapeseed

Zhang, L., Cheng, X., Chen, D. et al. Integration of bulked segregant analysis and transcriptome
sequencing reveals an interaction network associated with cluster buds trait in Brassica napus.
Theor Appl Genet 138, 200 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1007/s00122-025-04989-7

## Slide 67 — QTL-mapping

QTL-mapping
- Mapping where the “locus” is on the genome that cause the
phenotype or “trait” is called quantitative trait loci mapping (QTLmapping)
- After sequencing the two pools (positives and negatives) and having
the reads of both positive and negative parent, one can do QTLmapping
- Use program such as QTL-seq, see example qtlseq -r reference.fasta
- p parent.1.fastq.gz,parent.2.fastq.gz
- Simple yet effective
- b1 bulk_1.1.fastq.gz,bulk_1.2.fastq.gz
- b2 bulk_2.1.fastq.gz,bulk_2.2.fastq.gz
- n1 20
- n2 20
- o example_dir
