# Course information — Genomic Architecture 2026

> Converted from `course-info.docx` and `assessment-method.html`.

This course is aimed at introducing and familiarizing MSc Biology students with important and wide-spread concepts of architecture, structure and function of genomes of viruses, bacteria (mainly prokaryotes) and eukaryotes. The course is also open to interested students from other, related MSc programs.
Genome architecture determines many features and characteristics of all living organisms, and of viruses. Not only genetic characteristics and inheritance are governed by the build-up and structure of genomes. Also many features as diverse as function, control and expression of genes, the role of non-coding DNA and RNA, jumping genes, mobile genetic elements, evolution and speciation, are all governed by and dependent on the architecture of the genome(s) involved.
This course is about structural and functional concepts, not about nitty gritty details. Various important aspects of genome architecture and its role in functioning and evolution of organisms will be presented by different experts in the field. You will see aspects and examples from viruses, via bacteria and simple eukaryotes to more complex multicellular eukaryotes. Important aspects of human genomes and genetics will also be presented. In addition, modern and state of the art concepts and technologies to study and analyse genome structure and function will be discussed. 
Take home messages of this course include all concepts discussed and highlighted during lectures, in the workshops and in powerpoint presentations, with support literature indicated to help understand and look into examples.
